## **Before the Unweaving**

- **Population:** 
  -  20–25 Million Citizens.
  -  10 Million Slaves & Foreigners 

- **Wealth:** 
  -  Rich,
  -  Iron, Mithril, Silver, Rare Metals.
  -  Industrial & Artisan Hub

- **Location:** 
 -  Three Day march from Coast on Imperial highway.

- **Society:** 
 -  Home to three Imperial Senators and multiple patrician families. 

The Anvil of [[Aleria]], [[Veridia]] stood in the shadow of a great mountain peak. From its depths, veins of iron and mithril spilled from mine shafts and galleries that tunneled for miles beneath. The ores from the belly of the mountain came in such purity and abundance that merchant the world over would place orders, and Veridian Steel was well sought after by the best artisans and blacksmiths. 

Yet [[Veridia]] was no soot stained pit town. The cities markets and Forum teamed with fine silks from Keldoran, and exotic meats and fruits from [[Ferrax]]. With wide straight streets around the cities heart, ending in plazas adorned with fountains and statues denoted Alerian triumphs, and the Gods themselves. Amphitheaters held games, while colonnades shaded spellwrights and philosophers alike. 

The cities population swelled over the centuries; Imperial Census would put the figure between 20 to 25 Million Citizens, with another 10 million Slaves and Foreigners. The common citizenry was a mingling of miners, artisans, merchants, and foreign traders; with crowded tenement houses lining most streets. The Nobility was well entrenched, with villas of older houses near the heart of the city, and some newer nobility taking entire tenement blocks to create their own manor houses. Patrician families kept townhouses, with many doubling as Guild Halls, or Manufactorums. While three Imperial Senators resided within the city, their villas sprawled into the foothills at the edge of the mountain. 

[[Veridia]] was a city of two worlds, of Iron and of Silk; where a well off mithril miner may rub shoulders with nobles while watching games in one of the many marble amphitheaters. 




# The Unweaving


### A Night of Blight.

 It began a little after sunset, when the last warmth of the day still lingered to the stone facades of Veridia. The horizon blackened with what looked like a storm rolling in, but its clouds churned a deep red, veined with lightning that sparked violet and green. 

By the time horns sounded in the watchtowers, the wave was already rolling over the first wards at the edge of the city. 

When the storm hit, it carried no rain, nor wind. Only a sound like stone grinding against stone, as if the very foundations of the world were breaking apart. 


### The Shriven.

Those who were caught in the storm did not die outright. They staggered, clutching at their heads, and chests. Some fell in the streets, their bodies contorting and convulsing; others were able to flee into alleys, into their homes, or inside whatever door would let them in.

At first, confusion. Words failed in their mouths, and sight failed them as their eyes slowly burnt a dark hue of purple. Many began to lose their teeth, simply falling out in bloody chunks.

By midnight, their flesh sloughed from their bones, and hardened at once. Their burnt out eyes sinking into their skulls, and newly grown sharpened teeth erupted from their mouths. Yet as they transformed into grotesque abominations of their former selves; a father would call out to their child, a wife would beckon her lover closer.

Their purpose was singular. A bite, a wound, even prolonged touch would begin the process.

By dawn, most of Veridia were dead, or corrupted. 

### The Days After.

In the first nights, chaos reigned. 

People fled to the mountains, the rivers, and towards the sea. Caravans stampeded down the Imperial Highway. Yet the Corruption had already taken seed in many, and despite their frantic attempts to flee, they turned, or were taken by those who had done so already.

In a single night, Veridia fell; with no word from anywhere else from within the Empire.


### Domus Aurea, The Last Refuge.


[[Lucius Marcellan]] was a man of ancient patrician stock; known more for his patronage of games and feasts, than for his political or military prowess. The night of The Unweaving, he found himself on his usual nightly constitutional with his wife, and daughters. While he walked the walls, the trio of women would follow from the garden, and politely listen to him as he waxed on about the latest Senatorial affairs. 

Yet a cry came from one of the gate guards, a young lad, who was not yet fit enough to join the Legions. His voice cracked in terror as he pointed towards the horizon, as the first wisps of the storm boiled over the rooftops of [[Veridia]]. 

As the Unweaving came, it rolled around the mountain like a wave breaking against a cliff. Forks of lightning could be seen tearing swaths of stone off buildings below, and the storm a deafening roll of a thousand furnaces roaring at once. 

When the storm subsided after several long moments, the first screams carried up from the city, and [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] ordered the gates shut. And yet despite the guards hesitation, the thick iron doors of the [[Domus Aurea]] swung shut.

All through the night, the villa shook with sound. Shrieks of terror, the clash of steel, and the howls of the Shriven echoed up the mountain as they hunted. [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] and his family locked themselves in the Atrium, surrounded by the household guards, and the overnight staff. None slept, as they could all hear both claw, bone, and nail, scrape against the outer walls before dawn.

When dawn finally broke, the screams and desperate cries of survivors rose with the sun. hands slapped against the granite walls, and the gate rung with the sound of a dozen fists.  Families, miners, artisans, and nobles alike pleaded for shelter and safety. Yet their desperate cries only brought the attention of The Shriven; as they began loping towards the crowd like hounds to the hunt; tearing into the stragglers at the rear of the mass. 

It was not [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] who gave the order to open the gates, as he and his wife urged those around them to remain silent and hidden. 

Bare chested, and still clutching the sword he had seized from a guard in the night, [[Gaius Marcellan]] led the house guard to the gatehouse. 

He organized the hundred odd men into rank and file, creating a flowing channel of braced shields, spears, and flesh; and as he lifted the great oak plank that barred the gate shut, the great iron doors swung open as survivors flooded into the courtyard, trampling one another in their desperation. 

In the mad dash, thousands made it to safety within the walls; yet directly behind them the Shriven followed, fast and ravenous. Before the gates could be closed, flesh met steel, as the first rank of guards were pulled down screaming, torn apart before their comrades could kill the abominations before the gates could closed once more; and after a bloody struggle, the iron slammed shut once more.


### The Burrows.

The Villa was not built to hold more than a few Legions at its height. Within the first week, food stores ran low, and chambers overflowed with the sick and dying; yet everyday more refugees would make their way to the gates of the Fortress-Villa. 

The mountain was already honeycombed with mineshafts, but now, desperate laborers began to expand them, digging new avenues, connecting galleries, and entire side chambers within the stone.

The deeper they dug, the safer the felt; yet stranger still, they the constant tug of corruption waned. Excavated stone was dragged to the surface, and piled into barricades, at first inside the courtyard, and to reinforce the wall; later efforts pushed the walls out further, barricading the streets leading towards the Fortress-Villa.


### Consolidation.

At its peak, perhaps a few million souls reached the walls of [[Domus Aurea]]. And of those, many die before reaching the gates, ripped apart by Shriven and other beasts in the dark; while others who get inside fall victim to overcrowding, disease, hunger, and infighting. 

Within the first few days [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] was awe struck and dumbfounded; unable to speak for nearly a week from the shock of what has happened; leaving [[Julia Marcellan]] and [[Gaius Marcellan]] to lead; as two separate factions form under each. Yet at this time they are united, as [[Julia Marcellan]] enacts a strict martial law, which [[Gaius Marcellan]] enforces with strict adherence . 

Food and water are rationed, thieves publicly executed, and those able fit enough to labor are forced excavate the tunnels, or join the growing militia.

By many accounts, nearly half a million survive the first year.
