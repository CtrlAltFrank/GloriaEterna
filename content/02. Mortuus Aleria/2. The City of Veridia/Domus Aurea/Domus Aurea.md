### Domus Aurea, The Last Refuge.


[[Lucius Marcellan]] was a man of ancient patrician stock; known more for his patronage of games and feasts, than for his political or military prowess. The night of The Unweaving, he found himself on his usual nightly constitutional with his wife, and daughters. While he walked the walls, the trio of women would follow from the garden, and politely listen to him as he waxed on about the latest Senatorial affairs. 

Yet a cry came from one of the gate guards, a young lad, who was not yet fit enough to join the Legions. His voice cracked in terror as he pointed towards the horizon, as the first wisps of the storm boiled over the rooftops of [[Veridia]]. 

As the Unweaving came, it rolled around the mountain like a wave breaking against a cliff. Forks of lightning could be seen tearing swaths of stone off buildings below, and the storm a deafening roll of a thousand furnaces roaring at once. 

When the storm subsided after several long moments, the first screams carried up from the city, and [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] ordered the gates shut. And yet despite the guards hesitation, the thick iron doors of the [[Domus Aurea]] swung shut.

All through the night, the villa shook with sound. Shrieks of terror, the clash of steel, and the howls of the Shriven echoed up the mountain as they hunted. [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] and his family locked themselves in the Atrium, surrounded by the household guards, and the overnight staff. None slept, as they could all hear both claw, bone, and nail, scrape against the outer walls before dawn.

When dawn finally broke, the screams and desperate cries of survivors rose with the sun. hands slapped against the granite walls, and the gate rung with the sound of a dozen fists.  Families, miners, artisans, and nobles alike pleaded for shelter and safety. Yet their desperate cries only brought the attention of The Shriven; as they began loping towards the crowd like hounds to the hunt; tearing into the stragglers at the rear of the mass. 

It was not [[Lucius Marcellan|Lucius]] who gave the order to open the gates, as he and his wife urged those around them to remain silent and hidden. 

Bare chested, and still clutching the sword he had seized from a guard in the night, [[Gaius Marcellan]] led the house guard to the gatehouse. 

He organized the hundred odd men into rank and file, creating a flowing channel of braced shields, spears, and flesh; and as he lifted the great oak plank that barred the gate shut, the great iron doors swung open as survivors flooded into the courtyard, trampling one another in their desperation. 

In the mad dash, thousands made it to safety within the walls; yet directly behind them the Shriven followed, fast and ravenous. Before the gates could be closed, flesh met steel, as the first rank of guards were pulled down screaming, torn apart before their comrades could kill the abominations before the gates could closed once more; and after a bloody struggle, the iron slammed shut once more.


### The Burrows

The Villa was not built to hold more than a few Legions at its height. Within the first week, food stores ran low, and chambers overflowed with the sick and dying; yet everyday more refugees would make their way to the gates of the Fortress-Villa. 

The mountain was already honeycombed with mineshafts, but now, desperate laborers began to expand them, digging new avenues, connecting galleries, and entire side chambers within the stone.

The deeper they dug, the safer the felt; yet stranger still, they the constant tug of corruption waned. Excavated stone was dragged to the surface, and piled into barricades, at first inside the courtyard, and to reinforce the wall; later efforts pushed the walls out further, barricading the streets leading towards the Fortress-Villa.

[[Domus Aurea]] had always been an oddity of [[Veridia]], part Senatorial estate, and part Legion redoubt. 

Its foundations were dug deep into the Mountain centuries ago, during an era when [[Veridia]] had been a border city. Over time, the upper works of the Castra were beautified into marble courtyards and terraced gardens; while underneath tunnels carved to bedrock, cisterns were fed by mountain springs, and old mineshafts lay dormant.
